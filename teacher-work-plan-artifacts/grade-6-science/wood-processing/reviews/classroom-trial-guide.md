## 4. Privacy and aggregate evidence

## 5. Preparing the trial

Confirm the complete printed card set, worksheet, answer key, paper-only boundary, absence of tools and physical wood samples, accessible card alternatives, supervision and stop signals.

## 6. Part 1 observation procedure

Observe one 45-minute card-sequencing lesson. Record timing, transitions, comprehension, raw/intermediate/product classification, chain ordering, pathway comparison, evidence versus inference, unsupported-claim rejection, explanations and material usability.

## 7. Data-quality observations

Check whether learners use supplied card facts, label unsupported inference, reject origin or sustainability claims and state one limitation. Do not reconstruct missing observations as if directly seen.

## 8. Timing and transition evidence

Record actual duration and aggregate transition issues. Note whether sorting three pathways fits the planned time without deleting the limitation discussion.

## 9. Safety, stop conditions and incidents

Stop if tools, blades, machine demonstration, physical wood with splinter or dust risk, glue, solvent or heating enters the activity, or if supervision or accessibility fails. Incidents are separate from learning outcomes.

## 10. Findings and required changes

Link every partly met dimension to an exact minor finding and plan. Open major or blocking findings prevent a positive decision. Affected paths stay inside this artifact root.

## 11. Trial decisions

Only analysed evidence may yield `successful`, `successful_with_notes`, `repeat_trial_required` or `unsuccessful`. A positive trial does not establish comparative effectiveness.

## 12. Fingerprint invalidation

Material changes invalidate prior trial evidence. A future record must match the current fingerprint and current prerequisite reviews.

## 13. Registration of an analysed record

Register only immutable analysed records. Supersession is graph-derived; draft or conducted records do not support readiness, and unrelated active terminals fail closed.
